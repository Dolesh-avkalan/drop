## Question

Write a 250-word essay on why lighthouses were built. End with a line that says exactly: END-OF-ESSAY

## Answer

For centuries, lighthouses have stood as solitary sentinels on coastlines, their purpose rooted in the fundamental need to protect lives and property at sea. Before modern navigation technology, sailors relied on visible landmarks and the stars to determine their position. At night or during storms, however, coastlines became deadly traps. Rocky shores, hidden reefs, and narrow harbor entrances were invisible until it was too late. Lighthouses were built to solve this problem by providing a constant, recognizable beacon of light that could be seen from miles away.

The earliest lighthouses, such as the Pharos of Alexandria, used open fires to warn ships of danger. Over time, oil lamps, Fresnel lenses, and eventually electric lights made the beams brighter and more focused. Each lighthouse was placed at a strategic point—often on a headland, island, or pier—to mark a specific hazard or guide vessels into a safe channel. Distinctive flash patterns and colors allowed mariners to identify individual lighthouses and determine their location along a coast.

Beyond navigation, lighthouses also served as military and communication posts in some regions. Yet their core mission remained unchanged: to turn darkness and uncertainty into safety and direction. Though GPS and electronic charts have reduced their role, lighthouses remain symbols of human ingenuity and care for those at sea. They were built because a single light could mean the difference between shipwreck and safe passage. END-OF-ESSAY
