## Question

Write a 250-word essay on how trains changed travel. End with a line that says exactly: END-OF-ESSAY

## Answer

Before the railway age, land travel was slow, costly, and uncomfortable. Coaches rattled over rough roads at a few miles per hour, and journeys we now measure in hours took days or weeks. Trains changed that forever. By the mid-nineteenth century, steam locomotives were carrying passengers at unprecedented speeds, shrinking distances and reordering everyday life. A trip that once demanded exhaustion and expense became something ordinary people could attempt.

Railways also democratized travel. Cheaper fares encouraged migration, tourism, and commuting, while standardized timetables introduced a new discipline of punctuality. Towns along the tracks flourished; remote regions gained access to markets, news, and ideas. The train station became a gateway to opportunity, and the journey itself—once a hardship—became part of the experience, with dining cars, sleeper berths, and panoramic windows.

Freight transformed too. Goods moved faster and in greater volume, lowering prices and linking farms, factories, and ports into national economies. In many ways, the train built the modern world by making distance less decisive. Later, cars and planes offered new freedoms, but they inherited the expectations railways created: speed, reliability, and access for all. Travel would never again be limited to the pace of a horse.
