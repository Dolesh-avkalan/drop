## Question

Write a 250-word essay on how maps changed exploration. End with a line that says exactly: END-OF-ESSAY

## Answer

Maps fundamentally transformed the nature of exploration, turning it from a series of blind voyages into a cumulative science. Before reliable cartography, exploration was largely a matter of luck and oral tradition. Sailors navigated by stars, winds, and memory, but knowledge of distant lands remained fragmented and mythical. The introduction of portolan charts in the medieval Mediterranean, followed by Ptolemy’s rediscovered Geography in the Renaissance, gave explorers a spatial framework—however imperfect—to plan routes, estimate distances, and communicate findings.

As mapmaking improved, each expedition built on the last. Columbus carried a chart based on Toscanelli’s calculations (which underestimated the Earth’s circumference). Later, the systematic mapping of coastlines by Cook, Tasman, and others allowed empires to project power and commerce across oceans. Maps also became instruments of imagination: blank spaces on a map—terra incognita—beckoned explorers to fill them, while accurate charts reduced the terror of the unknown.

Crucially, maps shifted exploration from heroic individual journeys to collaborative, state-sponsored enterprises. A returner’s logbook could be worthless without a chart to place discoveries. Conversely, a good map made previous voyages obsolete. In this way, maps didn’t just record exploration; they dictated its direction, pace, and purpose. They turned the world from a maze into a grid, and in doing so, made the unknown reachable.
