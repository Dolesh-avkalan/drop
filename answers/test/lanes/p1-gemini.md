## Question

Write a 250-word essay on why lighthouses were built. End with a line that says exactly: END-OF-ESSAY

## Answer

Lighthouses have stood for centuries as sentinel beacons along perilous coastlines, serving a singular, vital purpose: to guide mariners safely through treacherous waters. Before the advent of modern satellite navigation and radar, the sea was an unpredictable expanse where hidden reefs, rocky shores, and fierce storms spelled disaster for unsuspecting ships.

The primary catalyst for building lighthouses was the urgent need to prevent shipwrecks and protect the immense economic value of maritime trade. As global commerce expanded, ports grew busier, and the loss of a single cargo vessel could devastate a merchant family or destabilize local economies. By projecting a powerful beam of light visible from miles away, lighthouses acted as early warning systems, alerting captains to shallow waters, hazardous headlands, and the safe approach to harbors.

Beyond commerce, lighthouses embodied a profound humanitarian mission. They were literal lifelines that saved countless sailors from drowning in cold, unforgiving seas. In eras past, keepers tended fires and oil lamps through howling gales, enduring isolation to keep their lights burning bright against the darkness. Ultimately, lighthouses were built not merely as architectural landmarks, but as symbols of hope, human ingenuity, and the enduring quest to make the treacherous oceans safer for travel.
