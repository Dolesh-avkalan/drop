## Question

A bat and a ball cost 1.10 in total. The bat costs 1.00 more than the ball. Then a second bat costs twice the first bat. What do the ball, the first bat and the second bat cost? Show the algebra briefly, using LaTeX for the equations.

## Answer

Let the ball cost $x$, the first bat cost $y$, and the second bat cost $z$.

$$x+y=1.10$$

The first bat costs $1.00$ more than the ball:

$$y=x+1.00$$

Substitute:

$$x+(x+1.00)=1.10$$$$2x=0.10$$$$x=0.05$$

So the ball costs $\$0.05$, and the first bat costs:

$$y=0.05+1.00=1.05$$

The second bat costs twice the first bat:

$$z=2(1.05)=2.10$$

Therefore:

$$\boxed{\text{Ball }=\$0.05,\quad \text{First bat }=\$1.05,\quad \text{Second bat }=\$2.10}$$
